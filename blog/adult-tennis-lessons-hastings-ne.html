Why One-on-One Coaching Actually Works for Adults

Group lessons can be great for kids who just need to be around other kids hitting balls. For adults, especially adults who are brand new or returning after years away from the game, one-on-one time with a coach makes a real difference.

When it's just you and Aden on the court, the entire hour is built around what you need. If your backhand falls apart when you're under pressure, that's what gets worked on. If you've never held a racket before and need to start from the absolute beginning with grip and stance, that's where the lesson starts. There's no racing to keep up with a group or pretending you understand something you don't.

Aden coaches all skill levels. That phrase gets thrown around a lot, but in this case it's straightforward — if you're a complete beginner, he'll meet you there. If you played in high school twenty years ago and want to rebuild your mechanics, he can work with that too.

What to Expect From Your First Lesson

First things first: you don't need to arrive with any specific gear to book. Once you've confirmed your session, you'll want a racket (many sporting goods stores carry decent beginner options, or ask Aden when you book if you have questions about what to bring) and athletic shoes with non-marking soles if you have them.

The booking system on tennistmate.com uses Setmore for scheduling, which means once you pay and confirm, your spot is locked in. Show up a few minutes early if you can — Aden mentions this on the site, and it's genuinely just a nice way to start. Get a feel for the court, loosen up a little, and you'll get more out of the full 60 minutes.

The first lesson tends to be part assessment, part introduction to mechanics. Aden is focused on building solid fundamentals and modern stroke mechanics — not just keeping the ball in play, but helping you actually understand why you're hitting it the way you are. That foundation matters more than most beginners realize.

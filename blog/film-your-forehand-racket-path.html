Film Your Forehand: The One Camera Angle That Fixes Your Topspin

By Aden Ingwerson | Hastings College Men's Tennis

You cannot feel your own swing. I mean that literally. Every week a player tells me their forehand feels "smooth" or "wristy" or "late," and then we film it, and what's on the screen has almost nothing to do with what they described. Your body lies to you at 70 miles per hour. The camera doesn't.

This exact topic is lighting up the Talk Tennis boards again this week: high-speed video breakdowns of the forehand racket path. And the consensus from the film matches what I see coaching every day, so let's make it useful.

Topspin Is a Direction, Not a Muscle

Here's what the slow-motion footage keeps proving: a real topspin drive is a mix of forward and upward racket path through contact. Not one or the other. Players who spray long are almost always too flat — nearly pure forward. Players who hit heavy, loopy sitters that get crushed are too vertical — all lift, no drive.

The good news is that this blend is visible. You don't need a biomechanics lab. You need one camera angle:

  • The side view. Set your phone on the fence, level with your contact point, perpendicular to the baseline. Film 10 rally forehands.
  • Watch the racket head from drop to contact. You want to see it travel low-to-high and back-to-front at the same time — a diagonal, not an elevator and not a punch.
  • Check the finish honestly. If the racket goes straight up like you're raising your hand in class, you're lifting, not driving. If it finishes at shoulder height with zero climb, you're why the ball keeps landing on the back fence.

The 10-Minute Fix

Once you can see your path, you can steer it. If you're too flat, exaggerate the drop: let the racket head fall below the ball before you swing, then chase up through it. If you're too vertical, move your contact point further in front — the further out front you meet the ball, the more your swing naturally flattens into drive. Film another 10 balls. Compare. That feedback loop — swing, film, adjust — will fix your forehand faster than a season of guessing.

Meanwhile, in Gear Land: The Wilson Python

The other thread you can't scroll past this week: the Wilson Python, dropping this October. Early hands-on reports say it looks much better in person than in the leaked photos, and the 98 vs. 100 debate is already raging. My favorite take from the thread, and I'm stealing it as coaching gospel: stop shopping by headsize. Two 100-square-inch frames can play nothing alike — look at the whole spec picture (weight, balance, stiffness) instead. Your strings and your swing path will do more for you than a new frame ever will. But if you're going to window-shop anyway, at least now you know what to look at.
